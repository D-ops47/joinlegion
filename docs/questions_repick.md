# Repick — four questions built around the struggle

## What I got wrong

My last set asked about temperament: where yesterday went, how you'd feel if it
ran without you. Those questions serve a **label**, not the person. They give
the answerer nothing while they're answering, and they tell us nothing about
what to build.

Given the audience is now **all business owners and executives** and the agent
exists to **assist with their major struggle**, the questions have to do one
thing: locate the struggle precisely enough to build against it.

That means the sequence is not a personality test. It is an **intake**.

---

## The design principle: narrow, then specify

Four questions, each one narrowing:

1. **Where** the struggle lives (domain)
2. **What shape** it takes (the failure mode)
3. **Why it persists** (what's been tried)
4. **What it costs** (the stakes, and the priority)

Each answer is useful to the person *as they give it* — that is what makes it
"value-given." By question four they have articulated a problem they may never
have stated plainly, which is the actual product of the exercise. The card then
reflects it back sharper than they said it.

Critically: **none of these are industry-specific.** A hospital executive and a
roofing contractor can both answer all four honestly, which the old set could
not claim.

---

## Screen 1 — Superpower *(free text, unchanged)*

> **What's your superpower?**

Stays. It is the one thing that makes the card feel like theirs, and it anchors
every agent prompt.

---

## Screen 2 · Q1 — What's the one thing that, if it were handled, would change everything? *(5)*

This is the struggle question. It replaces four temperament questions with one
direct ask, and it is the question every executive already has an answer to.

| Option | Domain |
| --- | --- |
| Getting more of the right customers | **demand** |
| Making the money work — pricing, margin, cash | **money** |
| Getting my time back | **time** |
| Getting my people to perform without me | **people** |
| Knowing what's actually going on in the business | **visibility** |

**Why these five:** they are the only five things an owner or executive ever
loses sleep over, at any scale. Demand, money, time, people, information.
Industry-neutral by construction.

---

## Screen 3 · Q2 — Why hasn't it been fixed yet? *(5)*

The highest-value question in the set, and the one no quiz asks. The answer
determines whether an agent can help *at all*, and if so, in what mode.

| Option | What it means for the agent |
| --- | --- |
| I know what to do, I just never get to it | **execution agent** — it does the work |
| I don't actually know what the right move is | **advisory agent** — it decides with them |
| I've tried, it didn't stick | **systems agent** — it maintains the habit |
| It depends on other people doing their part | **accountability agent** — it chases and follows through |
| I can't see it clearly enough to fix it | **diagnostic agent** — it surfaces the truth first |

This is the axis that actually determines what to build. Two people whose
struggle is "getting my time back" need completely different agents if one never
gets to it and the other has tried three times and watched it collapse.

---

## Screen 4 · Q3 — Which of these would you hand over tomorrow if you could? *(5)*

Names the concrete work. Deliberately phrased as *hand over*, not *what takes
time* — it asks what they are willing to release, which is a different and more
honest answer.

| Option | Work type |
| --- | --- |
| Chasing people — leads, replies, follow-ups | **pursuit** |
| Writing things — emails, docs, posts, proposals | **writing** |
| Deciding things — priorities, tradeoffs, judgment calls | **decisions** |
| Tracking things — numbers, status, who owes what | **tracking** |
| Talking to people — meetings, updates, check-ins | **comms** |

**Why "hand over":** an executive who says they'd hand over *decisions* has told
you something profound and slightly uncomfortable. That is a better signal than
any hours-based question.

---

## Screen 5 · Q4 — If nothing changes in twelve months, what happens? *(4)*

The stakes question, and the closer. It puts them in the cost right before the
card offers the fix — and unlike "what does it cost you when it slips," this one
scales from a solo operator to a CEO.

| Option | Priority it sets |
| --- | --- |
| We stall — same size, same problems | **momentum** |
| I burn out | **relief** |
| We lose ground to competitors | **speed** |
| I stay trapped in it forever | **autonomy** |

---

## How the agent gets built

The agent is now composed from **three axes**, not looked up from one:

```
struggle domain (Q1)  ×  why it persists (Q2)  ×  work to hand over (Q3)
                      +  priority (Q4)
```

Example composition:

> **Struggle:** getting my time back · **Why:** I've tried, it didn't stick ·
> **Hand over:** tracking things · **Stakes:** I burn out
>
> → **Your agent: the Systems Agent for Tracking**
> You don't need to be told what to do — you've done it. It collapsed because
> it depended on you remembering. This agent's job is to be the memory: it holds
> the numbers, the statuses and the open loops, and it reports without being
> asked. Its priority is your relief, so it does not add a single thing to your
> plate to maintain it.

**5 × 5 × 5 × 4 = 500 distinct agent specs**, all deterministic, all offline.

And the role — Creator / Technician / Visionary — is still derived, but from
these answers rather than measured separately. Someone handing over *decisions*
with a *people* struggle reads Visionary; someone handing over *tracking* who
knows what to do reads Technician. The label survives without spending questions
on it.

---

## Why this achieves the goal my last set missed

| Your requirement | How it is met |
| --- | --- |
| "value given" | Every question makes them articulate something useful; Q2 in particular is a question most owners have never answered out loud |
| "understand what agent they need" | Q2 determines the agent's *mode*, Q3 its *material*, Q1 its *domain*, Q4 its *priority* — that is a spec, not a label |
| "all business owners and executives" | Five universal struggles, no industry vocabulary anywhere |
| "assist with their major struggle" | Q1 *is* the major struggle, asked directly, first |
